# V2 maturity-aligned annual historical study

## Question and scope

Does replacing a one-observation variance extrapolation with a remaining-maturity forecast improve a cost-aware hypothetical option hedge? Does selecting the forecast first differ from jointly selecting the model and trading rule by net MSE or tail loss? These are separate questions; better predictive fit, lower transaction cost and lower downstream loss must not be conflated.

This is an executed six-currency study, using USD, JPY, GBP, CHF, CAD and AUD **units per EUR** from the unchanged ECB snapshot archived during V1. Reference rates are not executable quotes. Every currency shares a denominator and calendar shocks; the six series are not six independent market replications.

V1 already inspected USD/JPY outcomes for 2021–2025. All V2 2015–2025 annual results are therefore described as retrospective exploratory evaluation, despite computationally strict information timing. The additional January–September 2026 observations were in the archived raw file but were not performance-scored in V1. They were evaluated after `docs/v2_protocol.json` was frozen. This is source-level commitment, not external preregistration. No grid or model was revised in response to their outcomes, and their nine episodes per currency are too few for strong confirmation.

## Data provenance

`data/v2_source_manifest.json` records the original retrieval time, ECB source and reuse links, raw SHA-256, new six-column CSV SHA-256, protocol SHA-256, and quality checks. `data/v2_ecb_six_currencies.csv` retains observations from 1999-01-04 through 2026-09-30. The archived raw ZIP and all V1 processed data remain unchanged. No quotes are filled, interpolated, winsorized or corrected.

The historical download is a current vintage and may contain revisions. Causal algorithms do not remove vintage uncertainty. Published observations have unequal calendar gaps; annualization assumes 252 observations per year as a convention. Derived forecasts and normalized hedge calculations are project transformations and are not ECB analysis or endorsement.

## Target timing and purging

Let r_t = log(S_t / S_(t-1)). At decision t, the h-observation target is

`y_(t,h) = (r_(t+1)^2 + ... + r_(t+h)^2) / h`.

It is an average of future squared returns, not the square of their sum. Its interpretation as conditional variance requires negligible conditional mean. Each supervised row stores its first target date and last target date. The entire future target window must fall in the permitted training or scoring period.

For outer year Y:

1. Inner validation is calendar years Y-2 and Y-1.
2. Initial models use target windows starting no earlier than 2005-01-01 and ending strictly before January 1 of Y-2. Windows crossing that boundary are purged, even when the feature and first target precede it.
3. Alpha candidates 0.1, 1, 10 and 100 are compared using the same validation windows whose complete 21-observation target lies in the two validation years.
4. Ridge-flat fits h=1 and extrapolates its prediction to the remaining life. Ridge-term selects its alpha using h=21. That single selected term alpha is then used for each direct remaining-horizon regression h=1,...,21. There is no independent search over 21 alpha choices.
5. Validation hedge predictions are generated by these pre-validation fits. The validation model scores and hedge losses select three procedures at 5 bps.
6. Before outer prediction, both chosen model specifications are refitted using all training-start-onward labels whose last date precedes January 1 of Y. The outer year's outcomes cannot affect coefficients, standardization, multiplicative calibration, alpha or model/policy choices for that year.

Later years may legitimately learn from previously observed outer years. This is an expanding chronological procedure, not a permanently frozen estimator. Hyperparameter selection and strategy selection reuse inner validation data, making selected validation scores optimistic; the next outer period is computationally separate.

## Models and maturity alignment

The same seven V1 causal features are used: log trailing mean squared returns over 1, 5, 21, 63 and 126 observations, the trailing five-return sum, and the fraction of negative returns over 21 observations. No future-centered window is used.

- `rolling63`: trailing 63-return mean square, extrapolated flat.
- `ewma97`: recursive EWMA with fixed decay 0.97, extrapolated flat.
- `ridge_flat`: standardized Ridge on log one-observation target, annual alpha selection, training-only multiplicative mean-ratio calibration, extrapolated flat.
- `ridge_term`: direct log-target Ridge for each remaining horizon, with one shared annually selected alpha, separate training-only scalers and calibrations per horizon.

The Ridge transformation and correction are identical in definition to V1. Predictions are clipped to the prespecified daily-variance interval [1e-10, 0.04]. They are not guaranteed unbiased conditional forecasts. Fit metadata includes sample counts, exact last available labels, means/scales, coefficients, intercepts and correction factors.

At each hedge decision, an h-step forecast becomes `sqrt(252 * predicted_average_squared_return)` for the BS delta with h/252 remaining life. The target h decreases from 21 to 1 along the episode. This is a P-measure statistical forecast supplied to a BS control heuristic. It is not a market implied volatility surface, a Q-measure pricing model, or a proof of optimal replication under stochastic volatility.

## Common episode design and accounting

Each annual outer period contains 12 complete nonoverlapping 21-return episodes; the 2026 extension contains 9. The two-year inner validation period contains 24. Adjacent episodes share one boundary quote but never a return. Annual leftover returns are excluded from hedge episodes and recorded. Across the 11 retrospective years, each currency contributes 132 unique outer episodes. The six currencies collectively provide 792 currency episodes, which must not be treated as independent when pooling because their calendars and denominator overlap.

Each episode starts at normalized S=100, K=100, maturity 21/252, r=q=0, and a common BS call premium computed from the starting Rolling63 variance. All 4 model × 4 policy × 3 fee candidates share this exact premium and path. The data do not include actual options, actual financing curves, bid/ask spreads or execution delays.

Policies are daily, every five observations, and delta-centered constant no-trade bands of width 0.05 and 0.10. A band rebalance moves to the nearest boundary, not back to the target center. Entry starts at the center; expiry liquidates the stock and pays the call. These are explainable execution baselines, not optimal bands derived from an asymptotic theorem.

The shared ledger charges entry, interior trading and exit fees. P&L is short-option seller terminal net wealth. For identical positions, net P&L plus accumulated terminal-valued fees equals the zero-fee P&L. Tests independently verify this identity across all recorded historical fee scenarios.

## Three selectors

Every selector sees the same candidate class, common premium, validation paths and mean-cost budget of 0.12 per normalized initial spot 100:

- `forecast_first`: select a model by validation QLIKE against complete 21-return targets; within that model choose minimum validation MSE among cost-feasible policies.
- `joint_mse`: choose the cost-feasible model/policy pair with minimum validation net MSE.
- `joint_es90`: choose the cost-feasible pair with minimum empirical loss ES90.

If the applicable candidate set is infeasible, minimum mean cost is the explicit fallback; infeasibility is retained. Ties are deterministic. Choices are made at 5 bps and then frozen at 0 and 10 bps for sensitivity analysis. No test-driven reselection occurs. Feasibility is a mean validation criterion, not a pathwise cap or a future guarantee.

MSE is decomposed exactly into population P&L variance (ddof=0) plus squared mean P&L. Common premium mismatch remains part of both mean and tail outcomes. Adding fees back provides gross MSE but does not eliminate pricing-model bias. ES90 is the top 10% empirical seller-loss tail with fractional boundary mass. The 24 validation episodes represent only 2.4 equivalent tail observations; the 9 extension episodes only 0.9. Tail-driven selection is correspondingly fragile.

## Primary inference and multiplicity

The predefined historical contrast is Ridge-term minus Ridge-flat **squared net P&L**, using band05 at 5 bps. Negative favors the maturity-aligned method. For each currency, ordered complete outer episodes are paired on identical start/end dates. Noncircular overlapping moving-block bootstrap uses 4,000 resamples and block lengths 1, 3 and 6; block 3 is primary.

The approximate two-sided p-value resamples centered differences under a zero-mean null: it compares `abs(mean(d*))` for blocks from `d - mean(d)` with `abs(mean(d))`. A +1 numerator/denominator correction prevents reporting zero. It is **not** the fraction of uncentered percentile samples on the other side of zero. The 95% interval separately uses uncentered percentile resamples.

At block 3, Holm step-down adjustment is applied across six currency contrasts. Retrospective and 2026 contrasts are distinct reported families; 2026 remains exploratory. Holm can handle dependent tests when marginal p-values are valid, but these bootstrap p-values are only approximate. Structural breaks, annual model changes, endpoint weighting in noncircular blocks, discarded annual returns and tiny extension samples weaken the assumptions. Neither Holm nor more resamples repairs these issues.

These historical intervals do not rerun every model fit/selection inside each bootstrap replicate, and do not remove the investigator's retrospective design choices. The separate V2 simulation study repeats the complete finite-sample selection procedure to investigate that additional uncertainty.

## Executed findings and interpretation

All 72 currency-year folds, 6,912 candidate metric rows, 123,552 episode/model/policy/fee rows, 1,296 selector metric rows and 54,054 actual decision-prediction records were executed. The large row count is not an independent sample size. Complete representative ledgers contain 704 rows.

In the 2015–2025 primary block-3 comparison, all six 95% intervals include zero, and all six Holm-adjusted approximate p-values are 1. Maturity alignment therefore has not established consistent downstream improvement in this experiment. It sometimes helps the point estimate and sometimes hurts. The additional 2026 AUD comparison favors the flat model in the point estimate and has a block-3 adjusted approximate p-value around 0.042, but this relies on only nine episodes and must be read with block sensitivity and the stated limitations, not as a durable market effect.

Tail-objective selection does not uniformly deliver lower outer tail risk or lower MSE. Validation feasibility also does not prevent later average cost-budget violations. These negative results are retained; no model or policy was removed to improve the headline.

Run `python scripts/run_walkforward.py` from the project root to reproduce the fixed numerical experiment, then `python -m pytest tests/test_term_forecasting.py tests/test_walkforward_artifacts.py`. Reproduction is not a new independent confirmation. Changing the protocol, source or algorithm requires versioned provenance and explicit acknowledgment that all released outcomes are now known.
